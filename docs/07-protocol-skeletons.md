# Protocol skeletons

Five skeletons. Each defines the **shape** a raft-era suite must give an artifact for that artifact to do its job. None prescribe implementation. All use `<placeholders>` for anything specific to the reader's suite.

---

## PS-01 · Handoff frontmatter

Every cross-session, cross-audience, or cross-machine handoff document begins with the same five-line machine-readable frontmatter. Format below is illustrative; any equivalent shape (YAML, TOML, JSON header) is acceptable as long as all five fields are present. The anchor is detached: its digest excludes the anchor field itself, avoiding a self-referential hash.

```yaml
---
anchor_sha: <sha256 of this file excluding this anchor_sha line>
scope: <one-sentence handoff purpose>
date: <ISO date>
format_id: <handoff-shape name and version, e.g. auditor-review v1>
verify_status: <how the reader can independently confirm claims below>
---
```

- **anchor_sha** — a detached digest computed over the file except this field; recomputed on receipt by the reader, and failed closed if it differs
- **scope** — one sentence; a handoff that cannot state its scope in one sentence is trying to do too much
- **date** — ISO date; time zones become their own failure class, use UTC
- **format_id** — names the *shape*, not the audience; different audiences may share a shape
- **verify_status** — either "independent (see §Verification block)" or "author's word only" — force this to be honest

**Discipline**: a handoff that lacks any of these five fields is not a handoff; it's a message. Handoff-consuming check surfaces refuse to accept messages.

## PS-02 · Cold-read surface (`CURRENT` file)

A single file per suite that a cold-starting session reads to recover working state. Regenerated by a script from ground truth; **never hand-edited**.

Six-section shape:

```markdown
<!-- REGENERATED by <tool> at <utc>; do not edit; ground truth = <paths> -->
# <suite> · CURRENT

## 1 Authoritative version / phase
<what version, phase, or milestone the suite is at, as read from the single-authority source>

## 2 Recent history
<last N events from the append-only log, machine-rendered with kind + timestamp + bounded text>

## 3 Delivery / stage head
<the current head of whatever chain is active, plus its integration status>

## 4 Open items
<items requiring action, extracted mechanically from status flags; if none, say "none surfaced">

## 5 Next action
<either a single pointer, or an honest "there is no single next action, N owner rows bear on this"
 — never invent a priority order to make the section look tidy>

## 6 Verification block
<the commands a reader runs to independently verify sections 1-3>
```

**Design constraints**:

- All six sections **derived** from ground truth. Nothing hand-written.
- **Truncation is explicit**: text cut by length limits carries a visible marker like `…[truncated, full at <path>]`. Silent truncation is a failure mode (see FM-08 corollary).
- **Staleness declared**: the header states which sections are live-read and which decay. Sections that decay carry a budget rule ("if regenerated more than N days ago, rerun before trusting").
- **Section 5 must be honest**: if there is no single next action, say so.

## PS-03 · Owner decision queue

A single register where decisions that require a human owner are collected. The register is durable and ordered; additions leave prior rows intact so that the queue's history is inspectable across sessions.

Every row must carry, in whatever notation your suite chooses:

- **an opaque, stable identifier** — never reused, never renumbered
- **a pointer to the full context** — not a restatement; the queue is not a second store of truth
- **a target time or an explicit open-ended marker** — so that overdue rows are mechanically detectable
- **a delegation class** — drawn from a small closed vocabulary you define. At minimum, the vocabulary must distinguish "may proceed without the owner", "should proceed and inform the owner", and "may not be closed without the owner"
- **a status field with closed-set values** — including at least an open state and a closed state carrying a resolution note; a forwarded state is optional

**Discipline**:

- Rows are not deleted; closed rows remain for history.
- The delegation vocabulary is load-bearing. If a row marked "may proceed" is stuck waiting on the owner, the delegation was wrong; the correction is to revisit that row's delegation, not to override it in chat.
- A check surface scans for overdue open rows and flags them.
- Any actor tempted to ask the owner in chat for something derivable from disk instead appends a queue row and continues if their delegation allows.

**Reader-defined**: representation (table, YAML sequence, JSON array, one-file-per-row), identifier syntax, source-pointer form, exact delegation vocabulary, retention policy, and notification channel. The requirement is that a cold-starting session can enumerate open rows without reading the entire history.

## PS-04 · Role playbook

If your suite has multiple roles, each role gets a playbook. A playbook is a small, cold-readable contract that answers, at minimum, these six kinds of question — the exact section count, ordering, and encoding are the reader's choice:

- **Responsibility** — what this role does. If it takes more than a short paragraph, the role is too large; split it.
- **Completion evidence** — the finite conditions under which this role's work on a given item is considered done, expressed as things a check can verify.
- **Routing** — when this role finishes, where its output goes and to which downstream role.
- **Escalation** — the conditions that justify halting, and how to raise (which channel, what payload).
- **Risks** — observed failure shapes for this role. Each risk should be traceable to at least one prior incident that motivated its inclusion; speculative risks stay out until an incident justifies them.
- **Handoff** — the shape of the outbound artifact this role produces, as a template with placeholders. Actual dispatch mechanics live in whatever your suite's transport is.

**Rules**:

- Playbooks are documentation, not implementation. If a check surface exists that automates one role's outputs, that surface is a separate artifact.
- Playbooks are amended by appending, not by editing history. Anti-patterns marked speculative should be removed or downgraded once observed evidence contradicts them.

**Reader-defined**: section count and ordering, role names, transport, notation for incident traceability, and paste-ready command shapes.

## PS-05 · Scoped reconsideration

When a prior review or decision rejected an outcome on multiple grounds and new evidence addresses only some of those grounds, the next round is a **scoped reconsideration** — narrow, explicitly bounded, and independently verifiable. The pattern below states its requirements at the level of what a request must communicate; storage medium (a document, a ticket, a signed record) and response transport (a paste-back, a reply, an issue comment) are the reader's choice.

A scoped reconsideration request must carry:

- **A reference to the prior decision** — the exact document, identifier, or version whose verdict is being partially revisited. This is a citation, not a summary; the reviewer must be able to reach the prior verdict without human help.
- **A precise description of what has changed** — enumeration of new artifacts with sufficient provenance (path, hash, or equivalent) that the reviewer can independently verify them. State explicitly what has *not* changed since the prior verdict.
- **The position taken by the requester** — the rule being invoked, quoted verbatim; what is claimed satisfied and, critically, what is explicitly *not* claimed. Exception-invocations weaken the rule and are prohibited.
- **A finite list of review questions** — each answerable with a verdict from a small closed enum (resolved / partially resolved / still open, with optional qualification), plus one addressing overall status. If the verdict is not "resolved", the request may name the minimum additional evidence needed, which must be reachable inside the workspace and must not require weakening a rule.
- **An explicit out-of-scope list** — every other prior blocker enumerated with one-line status, and every asset the reviewer must not touch.
- **A response contract** — the exact location and structural shape of the reviewer's output, so the requester's round-trip is a save-and-verify rather than a formatting exercise.
- **A next-action statement** — what the requester will do if the verdict is not "resolved". The reviewer's job is to state the minimum, not to design the fix.

**Discipline**:

- **Never invoke exceptions to rules.** If evidence does not fit the rule's letter, say so; do not propose weakening the rule.
- **Never reproduce a verdict you have not read.** Cite it by path or identifier; do not paraphrase.
- **Load-bearing values appear in full.** Truncated identifiers or abbreviated hashes should not be the values a reviewer must verify against.

**Reader-defined**: section count and ordering (prose, numbered outline, or machine-readable header plus body), the digest or signature scheme for "sufficient provenance" (SHA-256, git commit hash, signed manifest, or equivalent), transport for the response contract, and the exact wording of the verdict enum — provided its cardinality remains small and closed.

## Non-goals of these skeletons

- They don't tell you what shape your *log* format takes (event JSON? YAML? plain text?). That's your call. They only require *whatever* your log is, it's append-only and machine-parseable.
- They don't tell you what your *state* file looks like. They only require: derived, single-authority.
- They don't ship the tools that produce these artifacts. That's `docs/07`-adjacent implementation work; the framework stops here.
